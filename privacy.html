playlist.lens

Privacy policy

Last updated 23 November 2026 · covers the playlist.lens website and the playlist.lens Android/iOS app

The short version. playlist.lens reads your Spotify data to show you stats about it. No ads, no analytics, no selling data. By default the server stores nothing about you. It only keeps data for three features: listening tracking (if you turn it on), crash reports from the app (you can turn these off), and share codes (if you make one).

Who runs this

playlist.lens is an independent, non-commercial hobby project. It is not made, endorsed or sponsored by Spotify. For any privacy question or request, open an issue at github.com/ItsAstroDude/playlist-lens-web/issues. Issues are public, so don't put personal details in them. Say what you need and we'll reply there.

What playlist.lens can see in your Spotify account

When you log in, Spotify asks you to allow these permissions:

  • your profile (display name, picture, country and subscription type)
  • your playlists, including private and collaborative ones, and your Liked Songs
  • what you're playing now, your recently played tracks, and your top artists and tracks
  • your devices and playback, so the app can start and queue music for you
  • permission to create and edit playlists. This is only used when you ask it to, for example saving a refreshed copy of a playlist

Almost all of this is fetched when you open a screen, shown to you, and not stored on our server. You can withdraw access at any time at spotify.com/account/apps.

What is stored, where, and for how long

On your device

  • Website: one cookie holds your Spotify sign-in for this browser. It is signed, can't be read by scripts on the page, and is only sent over HTTPS. It's strictly necessary for the site to work, so there's no cookie banner. Pressing disconnect removes the sign-in. There are no analytics or advertising cookies.
  • App: your Spotify sign-in is kept in the phone's secure storage. Analysis results, settings and any listening history you import from a Spotify data download are kept in the app's own storage on your phone. Photos you pick as custom covers stay on your phone. Logging out deletes all of this from the phone.

On our server

WhatDetailsHow long
Listening tracking
(app, off by default)		 If you turn on Listening tracking, the server saves your recent plays every hour so weekly and monthly stats are complete. It stores your Spotify user ID, an encrypted Spotify sign-in (refresh token), and for each play: the time, track, artists, album, cover-art link and length. Until you turn tracking off, which deletes the token and every saved play. When you log out, the app asks whether to stop tracking.
Crash reports
(app)		 When the app hits an error it sends the error message, technical stack trace, app version and update, platform (Android/iOS) and the screen you were on, linked to your Spotify user ID. Nothing else about you is included. Turn this off in Settings › About. The newest 500 per account. Delete them any time from Settings › About › Labs › Errors › Clear.
Share codes
(web and app)		 When you create a share code for your taste profile, the server stores the profile: your display name, top artists and genres, track/playlist/artist counts, audio averages and vibe label. Anyone who has the code can view it. Until it's pushed out by newer codes (the server keeps the newest 20,000) or the server's storage is reset. To have one removed sooner, open an issue (see above) with the code.
Basic request data Like any website, the server sees your IP address with each request. It's held in memory for up to a few minutes to limit abuse. Our host also keeps standard request logs (IP address, time, address requested). Rate-limit data: minutes. Host logs: per the host's retention.

Services we use

  • Spotify: your account data comes from the Spotify Web API. Spotify's own privacy policy applies to your Spotify account.
  • Render: hosts the server (United States).
  • Neon: the database for listening tracking and crash reports.
  • Expo: delivers app updates. The app checks Expo's servers for updates, which shows them your IP address and app/device details.
  • jsDelivr: the website loads its chart library from jsDelivr's network, so your browser contacts it.
  • Deezer, Apple (iTunes) and Spotify's public player: to find 30-second song previews, our server looks songs up by track ID, ISRC code or title and artist. Nothing about you is sent.
  • GitHub: a scheduled job starts the hourly tracking run. It carries no personal data.

Some of these providers are outside the UK and EU (mainly the United States). They process data under their own data-protection terms, including standard contractual clauses where those apply.

Why we use your data (legal basis)

  • Showing your stats, playlists and playback: to provide the service you asked for.
  • Listening tracking: your consent, which you give by turning it on and withdraw by turning it off.
  • Crash reports and abuse limits: our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and the server safe. You can turn crash reports off.
  • Share codes: your request when you create one.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the EU or UK), you can ask to see, correct, export or delete your data, object to how it's used, or complain to your data-protection authority. Most of this you can do yourself:

  • Turn off listening tracking: deletes all tracking data on the server.
  • Settings › About › Labs › Errors › Clear: deletes your crash reports on the server.
  • Log out or disconnect: deletes your data from the phone or browser.
  • Remove playlist.lens at spotify.com/account/apps: cuts off its access to Spotify.

For anything else, such as a share code or a copy of your data, open an issue as described above.

Children

playlist.lens isn't aimed at children. You need a Spotify account to use it, so Spotify's minimum age applies.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. Significant changes will be mentioned in the app's What's new.
